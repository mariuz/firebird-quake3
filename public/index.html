How it works

      
    
        
  • The BSP becomes tables. A Quake III map (IBSP 46) is copied in lump by lump: FACES with their
          plane and bounding sphere, FACE_VERTS with texture and lightmap coordinates (the Bézier patches tessellated
          into triangles), NODES, LEAVES with their cluster and a decompressed PVS as a hex string,
          LEAFFACES, and the collision geometry: BRUSHES and BRUSHSIDES with their planes and
          contents, indexed through LEAFBRUSHES — the patches add one-sided facets to them, as cm_patch.c does. Every
          MD3 is a row of MODELS; the entity lump becomes MAP_ENTS; bg_itemlist is ITEM_DEFS.
    • 
        
  • Collision is a recursive procedure. RHC is CM_TraceThroughTree: the moving box's
          extents push each split plane out, the segment is split at the plane and both sides are visited nearest first; in
          every leaf reached, CLIP_LEAF clips the segment against each brush and facet whose contents match the mask.
          TRACE_MOVE runs it against the world, every door and platform (brush models have a leaf of their own) and
          the players' boxes. FLY_MOVE (PM_SlideMove), WALK_MOVE (the 18-unit step), MOVE_STEP
          and PUSH_MOVE are bg_pmove.c and g_mover.c in PSQL.
    • 
        
  • The game is PSQL. Q3_TIC runs at 20 Hz: the player's ground, air and water moves, jumps and
          footsteps; doors, plats, buttons, bobbing and rotating platforms, pendulums and trains; jump pads that compute their
          launch velocity from their target, teleporters, hurt triggers; the items of bg_itemlist with their respawn times;
          the nine weapons from the gauntlet to the BFG10K; damage with armour and the quad, knockback, gibs; the obituaries,
          the frag count and the announcer; and the bots — see, chase, strafe, pick things up, shoot, die, respawn — in
          BOT_THINK. The bots find their way over a waypoint graph built when the map loads: a node at every
          spawn point, item, jump pad and teleporter and on a grid over every floor, an edge wherever a trace says a player
          can walk, step, drop, fly off a pad or teleport; WP_ROUTE is a breadth-first search in PSQL.
    • 
        
  • The renderer is a query. FRAME_ALL finds the leaf and cluster the eye is in and, once per
          cluster, marks every face of every leaf whose cluster is in its PVS (R_MarkLeaves) with its plane and bounding
          sphere. A frame is then a scan of the marked faces with the back-face and frustum tests as expressions, aggregated
          with LIST() into one row of face ids; the brush models add a row each at their own origin. The same
          result set carries the MD3 entities in the PVS with their pose and animations, the sounds, effects and console
          lines of the tic: one round trip per frame. The other renderer mode, FRAME_FACES, projects every
          vertex in SQL as well.
    • 
        
  • JavaScript only paints. Two painters share the same rows. The WebGL one is a port of Quake III's renderer: the world's vertices sit on the card, a shader multiplies the texture by the lightmap page and adds the glow stages with the stage's blendFunc and tcMods, the sky is the shader's cloud layers by pixel direction, the MD3 models are lit from the light grid in the vertex shader. The software one is a 32-bit framebuffer and a z-buffer; perspective-correct spans, divided every 16
          pixels, that multiply the texture by the lightmap page; triangle lists for the patches and meshes; MD3 models lit
          from the light grid, the player parts hung on their tags; the sky's cloud layers by pixel direction; additive and
          alpha blended surfaces; the status bar from the game's own pictures. Sounds are rows in SOUND_EVENTS,
          spatialised with the Web Audio API.
    •
